Loudness

1990Yours2026 Peak——— Gated RMS——— Crest———

Ribbon thickness is absolute level against one shared reference, so a louder master really is a fatter groove. Crest factor is peak minus gated RMS — the headroom a master leaves itself.

Both alternates are simulated. 1990 restores transients — a 1 ms peak follower against a 200 ms average, boosting whatever sticks out — plus shelf cuts of 2.5 dB below 70 Hz and 2 dB above 8 kHz, then peak-normalized to −1 dBFS. 2026 loud is the opposite: drive into a 0.5 ms brickwall limiter with a −0.3 dBFS ceiling, chasing a −9 dBFS average. Limiting cannot be perfectly undone, so 1990 shows the era's dynamics, not what an engineer would have printed.

Ribbon thickness is peak level, drawn asymmetrically — left channel toward the hub, right toward the rim. Color is the spectral center of that slice: bass runs blue, cymbals and sibilance run gold.

Real geometry: track pitch 1.6 µm, program area 25–58 mm radius (a 120 mm / 4.7 in disc), scanned at a constant 1.2 m/s. The Red Book standard is written in metric, so these are the figures as specified rather than converted. Radii here are true; the drawing compresses 20,500 revolutions into 28 visible turns.
